Clear diurnal cycle: night peak ~58 (00–02 h), afternoon trough ~41 (15–16 h); 24 h autocorrelation bump of 0.61–0.80.
Cross-station correlation 0.65–0.85 over the last 60 days: pool stations and use other stations' recent levels as features.
Station regimes differ: Borivali is stablest (recent std 9–12), Malad and Byculla are spiky, Shivaji Nagar is highest in winter (Nov 2025 mean 76 vs 27–44 elsewhere).
Seasonal drift: daily city mean fell from ~60 (mid-Oct) to 33–36 (late Oct / early Nov), climbing back to 45–49 by 5–6 Nov. November opens Mumbai's winter inversion season, so a slight upward drift is the prior.
available_at lag: median ~89 h between observation and availability. The 2–3 day gap is deliberate; validation must replicate it.
Alignment gotcha. Train timestamp_ist marks the end of the hour; Test IDs also use the end time (T0100 = start 00:00, end 01:00). Shift both or neither.
3. Validation design
Rolling-origin backtests that exactly mimic the final setup are worth more than any model choice.
Origin = day D at 07:00; forecast D+2 00:00 through D+3 23:00 (horizons 41–89 h).
Use 15–25 origins: every 2–3 days through Oct–Nov 2025 and Sep–Nov 2026.
Weight the Diwali-2025 analog origin heavily: 22 Oct 2025 07:00 forecasting 24–25 Oct 2025.
Score RMSE on raw (uncleaned) targets, as the organiser does; train on cleaned targets.
Fit ensemble weights on these backtests, never on the public leaderboard.
Never use random K-fold or a split without the 2-day gap; both look great and fail.
4. External data worth pulling
Two free, public sources are legitimately available at prediction time (forecasts issued before 11 Nov), so no leakage. If time is short, take the second one first.

5. The models, ranked
LightGBM on anchored lag, calendar and weather features is the primary model; CatBoost, a decayed-persistence baseline and a calibrated CAMS forecast are the ensemble partners.
Model A: LightGBM, direct multi-horizon, anchored lag features (primary)
Why best: gradient boosting on hand-built lag/calendar/weather features has won essentially every Zindi/Kaggle hourly-pollution competition. It handles mixed feature types, missing values natively, nonlinear interactions (hour × station × wind), and trains in seconds, so 20+ backtests fit before the deadline.
Framing: one pooled model across stations; each training row = (origin time, station, target hour) with horizon_h as a feature, built only from data available at origin.
Feature group
Features
Anchor (at origin)
Last observed value; mean/median/std/min/max of last 24 h, 48 h, 72 h; same-hour-of-day mean over last 3 and 7 days; last-24 h trend slope; hours since last valid observation

City-wide last-24 h mean; other 3 stations' last-24 h means
Calendar
Hour (sin/cos), day of week, weekend, day of year, days_since_diwali (plus 2025 analog), is_post_diwali_window
Station
Categorical ID; station climatology for that calendar month and hour
External (at target hour)
Weather forecast: wind speed/direction, RH, temperature, PBLH, precipitation; CAMS PM2.5
Target: cleaned pm25 on the raw scale. Log transform optimises the median and under-predicts means, which hurts RMSE. If spikes dominate, compare huber or tweedie objectives on backtests.
Starting parameters: objective=regression, learning_rate=0.03, num_leaves=31–63, min_data_in_leaf=50–100, feature_fraction=0.7, bagging_fraction=0.8, bagging_freq=1, lambda_l2=1–5, n_estimators≈1500–3000 with early stopping on the latest backtest fold, fixed seed. Average 3–5 seeds.
Model B: CatBoost on the same feature table (ensemble partner)
Why: ordered boosting and symmetric trees give a different bias than LightGBM. It tends to win on small, noisy tabular data (~48k rows, 4 stations) and handles station_id, hour and day-of-week as native categoricals. Typically 0.2–0.5 RMSE better alone than LightGBM on noisy series; the blend reliably beats either.
Parameters: loss_function=RMSE, depth=6–8, learning_rate=0.03, l2_leaf_reg=3–10, iterations≈3000 with early stopping, fixed random_seed, cat_features=[station, hour, dow].
Model C: Decayed-persistence × diurnal-profile baseline (RMSE safety net)
Why: at 41–89 h a well-tuned statistical forecast is hard to beat and nearly impossible to blow up. Prediction = per-station hourly profile (last 2–3 clean weeks) scaled to a level that mixes the station's last-72 h clean mean with its November climatology, the climatology weight rising with horizon (decay rate fitted on backtests). Add the post-Diwali multiplier learned from 2025 (+3/+4 days ≈ 0.9–1.0× baseline).
Zero over-fitting risk, zero leakage, five minutes to build. Its main value is pulling the tree models back toward sane levels when their lag features are contaminated by the Diwali tail.
Model D: Bias-corrected CAMS PM2.5 forecast
Why: a different information source, not just a different algorithm. A chemistry-transport model driven by weather has the most decorrelated errors of anything available, which is where ensembles gain most. CAMS is systematically biased at Indian urban stations (usually under-predicts), so fit a per-station linear or small-LightGBM calibration (CAMS value, hour, station → observed) on the historical archive. Also feed the CAMS value into Models A and B as a feature; if the archive download is slow, use it as a feature only.
Model E (optional, only if A–D are done by ~18:00): Ridge/ElasticNet, or Random Forest per the starter
Why: a linear model extrapolates smoothly where trees plateau, and is the natural stacker. Use it as a fourth blend member or as the meta-learner over A–D (fit on backtest out-of-fold predictions, non-negative coefficients). Skip deep learning (GRU, N-BEATS, TFT): 4 stations × ~20 months is too little data, 12 hours is too little time, and reproducibility review punishes flaky training.
6. The winning combination
Final blend, weights fitted on the rolling backtests (rough starting point), then clipped to [0, ~300]:
\hat{y} = 0.35\,\text{LightGBM}_{\text{seed-avg}} + 0.30\,\text{CatBoost} + 0.20\,\text{DecayedPersistence} + 0.15\,\text{CalibratedCAMS}
Post-processing that typically buys a few RMSE points:
Shrink toward station climatology by horizon: pred = α(h)·model + (1 − α(h))·clim, with α falling from ~0.9 at 41 h to ~0.7 at 89 h; fit α on backtests.
Night-time floor: 00–03 h predictions ≥ the afternoon ones for the same station and day (diurnal consistency).
Never predict 0 or anything above ~300 for 11–12 Nov. Expected reality is ~35–60 µg/m³.
